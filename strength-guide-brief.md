# Hipertrofia, Fuerza y Potencia — strength guide

**Status: v1 SHIPPED in Spanish, August 2026** — `/members/fuerza/`. EN/PT pending. Static explainer with one interactive element (a goal switch), modelled on `/members/carga/`.

**Source:** an Andy Galpin summary Iván supplied. The physiology is his and it is sound; ***the transfer to endurance athletes is the work this guide actually does***, and it is where the source needed correcting.

## The decision the whole guide turns on

The source opens its 12-week block with **four weeks of hypertrophy**. `methodology.md` line 19 says *"Max-strength protocol (3–5×3–5 heavy, 3' rest): max activation, **minimal hypertrophy**"* — a deliberate exclusion, because added mass is a cost in W/kg and running economy.

***Galpin's own text resolves it rather than contradicting it.*** He says you only need to separate the protocols "if you specifically want to get stronger *without* gaining muscle weight (e.g., competing in a weight class)." **That is exactly the endurance athlete's situation — they are in a weight class, it is just self-imposed.** The 12-week block simply was not written for them.

So: **the default path replaces the hypertrophy phase with a tissue-tolerance phase** (same slot, different intent and dose), and real hypertrophy lives behind a goal switch, off-season only, with the cost stated in a warm-coloured box — the only one on the page.

## What was corrected from the source

| Source said | Published as | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hypertrophy: 5–30 reps (prose), 8–12 / 30–80% / 30s–3min (table 1), 8–15+ / 1–2min (table 2) | **6–12 reps, 60–80%, RIR 1–3, rest 90s–2min** | The document contradicted itself three ways in one file. One set of numbers, with a note that the wide range is real and proximity to failure is what decides. |
| Power rest: 2–5 min (table 1) vs 3–5 min (table 2) | **3–5 min** | Same reason. |
| "3 to 5 days per week" of strength | **2/week as the norm; 3–4 during a max-strength phase** | Iván's call, Aug 2026. 3–5 does not survive 5h of endurance — fatigue, not time, is the constraint. *The nuance is his and it is the good part: max strength carries little fatigue because total volume is low, so it can be practised more often. Hypertrophy cannot.* |
| 12-week block as 4+4+4, no deloads | **Each 4-week phase = 3 loading + 1 easy** (2+1 for 50+) | Matches `methodology.md` §Blocks. The source's block has no recovery weeks at all. |

## What was added, because it was missing and matters more than anything in the source

- **Concurrent-training interference.** Same-day separation of ≥6h, never heavy legs the day before quality or a long session, stack gym onto an already-hard day rather than spoiling an easy one, and **max strength interferes least while hypertrophy interferes most**. This is the first question every endurance athlete asks when they start lifting, and the source does not mention it.
- **What strength actually buys an endurance athlete:** economy, tissue resilience, durability. *Explicitly not "more watts"* — the source leans on that phrasing and it promises something that will not happen.
- **Maintenance dose.** One session a week holds strength for weeks **if load stays high and volume drops**. The classic race-week error is the reverse — lighter weight, same volume — which maintains nothing and still costs freshness.
- **Four common errors**, ending with the one that matters most: abandoning the gym exactly when race volume climbs and tissue needs it most.
