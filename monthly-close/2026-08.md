## 2. What moved, and why

### The cost structure is not where anyone would look for it

**Channel cost $818.94 against total operating costs of $250.92.** *TrainingPeaks costs 3.3× what running the entire business costs — every server, subscription, certification and the LLC combined.*

**That reframes both parked initiatives.** *Direct checkout and the CoachMatch→Private migration are not margin polish; they are the only cost lever in this business with any size. §2.2 of the runbook noted the direct-checkout argument had "no number attached to it." It has one now, and it recurs monthly.*

***And the CoachMatch-vs-Private gap is wider than the 16.5% `pricing-and-positioning.md` states.*** *CoachMatch money lands in PayPal and costs a further 3.5% + $3 to move; TP Payments money settles into Mercury and is spent from there without moving. **~20 points, not 16.5** — and the difference is not a fee, it is that one channel's money arrives usable and the other's does not.*

### All-Access lost two of three subscribers, and blending hid it

**Coaching NRR 93.4%. All-Access NRR 27.3%. Blended, 91.6%** — which reads as a mild wobble in a healthy book.

*Reported as one number this would have been invisible. **Retention is now reported per product line, never blended** — the levers are different and so is the answer.*

🚨 ***The product is sold and not delivered.*** *The one remaining paying subscriber has never logged in. Of 33 paying coached athletes with tokens, **two** have ever opened the members area — 7 accesses between them. Of 157 total accesses, **94 are Iván's own QA accounts**.*

### 138 CoachMatch leads, 2 closes

**And the funnel says the problem is not price.** *Of 182 CoachMatch leads lost all-time:*

| Reason | n | share of losses |
|---|---|---|
| **No response** | 129 | **70.9%** |
| Not interested | 41 | 22.5% |
| **Price** | 12 | **6.6%** |

*`pricing-and-positioning.md` spends most of its length on $149 vs $99. **Price accounts for under 7% of losses; silence for 71%.*** *An unvoiced objection cannot be answered; a response rate can be worked.*

**The channels that cost no commission convert 5–7× better and produce almost no volume:** *REFERRAL 6 of 6 · WEBSITE_FORM 4 of 5 · COACHMATCH 33 of 253.* **That is the referral and website-capture case with numbers behind it for the first time.**

### Plan sales: flat traffic, a third of the conversions

*Marketplace views per day: May 68 · June 95 · **July 75 (pixel outage — 20 days lost, not a demand drop)** · August 94.* **August traffic is flat against June. June produced 23 sales; August produced 8.**

⚠️ ***Reported as counts, not a rate — 8 is below the ~10-event floor.*** *This is the directional peek the July 29–30 listing-rewrite experiment scheduled for ~Aug 30. **The direction is not encouraging and the decision date remains Oct 27**, per that experiment's own rule.*

### Reviews stopped

**July produced 9. August produced 0.** *The runbook argued the process "demonstrably works and demonstrably isn't running." August confirms the second half. Nothing was broken — the ask was not made.*

### Corrections this close produced

- 🚨 **The TrainingPeaks plan take rate is a flat 30.00%, not a drifting 28.6–29.7%.** *`amount` includes sales tax Iván never receives; the old denominator was tracking the country mix of each month's buyers. A real pricing change would have been indistinguishable from noise.*
- 🚨 **`agreementDate` never needed building.** *Twenty's `signUpDate` already is the day the athlete said yes; the trap the runbook warned about belongs to a different field with a near-identical name in `athlete_tenure.csv`.*
- 🚨 **The name-derived `athlete_id` is retired for `tpId`.** *A key derived from a field the athlete controls. Proof arrived in the data: one athlete is `Paula` in Twenty and `Padua` in TrainingPeaks.*
- **A comp is not a 100% discount.** *`revenueStatus` separates them; four free athletes recorded as discounts would have injected ~$596/month of phantom discount into the one figure the price fields exist to measure.*
