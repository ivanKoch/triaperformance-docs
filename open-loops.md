- **Content engine V2 — reducing the two review gates.** *(Scoped Aug 12, 2026. Not queued: the honest sequence puts distribution and the feedback loop first, since a review gate should only be loosened once there's evidence it's rubber-stamping.)*
  **The principle: don't remove a gate on a hunch — and the evidence already exists, unread.** `content_pieces` stores `original_body` alongside `body` specifically so the model's output can be diffed against what Iván approved. **Nobody has ever looked at it.** A near-zero edit rate across twenty `plan_guide` pieces is an argument for loosening; anything else isn't. That diff is the prerequisite for everything below.
  **Translation approval should go first, and it's the one that can go honestly.** You can't automate *"is this true?"* — but you can automate *"does this say the same thing as the article already approved?"* **Translation is the only stage with a ground truth to compare against.** A fidelity check could verify mechanically that the sibling names the same entities, carries identical physiological numbers, links plans of the same sport/distance, and matches section structure; flag only semantic drift, auto-approve the rest. That removes a third of the approvals, and the Valencia→CIM swap is exactly what it would have caught — the entity changed while everything around it stayed put.
  **Article approval shouldn't be removed, only made cheaper.** Today it means reading 1,400 words cold. A pre-flight report — every numeric claim extracted with whether it traces to `methodology.md`, plan cards listed with their metric/duration spread, assertions appearing nowhere in the KB — turns reading into checking. A second model as adversarial reviewer (annotating, not approving) would plausibly have flagged the 120–160 g/h carbohydrate figure.
  **The idea queue should shrink, not the gate.** *(Iván's observation, Aug 12.)* The research agent predates the translator and still proposes EN and PT ideas for universal topics the translator now covers free. Approving an EN idea *and* letting an ES article translate produces **two English articles competing on the same topic**. EN/PT ideas are only worth approving when genuinely market-specific — a US race, a Portuguese-catalogue constraint. Restricting generation accordingly would cut the queue meaningfully.
