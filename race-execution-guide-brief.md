# Race Execution Guide — home doc

*Last updated: September 11, 2026*

**Home doc for `race-execution-guide/guia-de-ejecucion-{es,en,pt}.md`**, the athlete-facing guide to preparing, executing and deciding a race.

**Owns:** the scope decision, the cut list, the reframes, the four templates, and the sources behind every external figure.

**Owns no numbers.** Every pacing rule, taper structure and debrief step in the guide is a copy of `methodology.md` §9; every pain rule is a copy of §7; the referral boundary is a copy of §11. Move it in `methodology.md` first, then copy it here. **Owns no fuelling content at all** — `fueling-guide-brief.md` and `methodology.md` §8 own that, and the guide cross-references rather than restating, including the hyponatremia block.

---

## 1. What happened

A *Kit de Herramientas Mentales* existed — a long mental-skills guide covering growth mindset, CBT and ACT worksheets, mantras, imagery, box breathing, a body scan, and a section headed "Depresión Post-Carrera."

Two independent AI critiques (Claude, Grok), run separately against the same file, converged on the same verdict without knowing about each other: **this was a scope problem, not a writing problem.** A performance coach was distributing named psychotherapy protocols and a section about depression, under his own name, with no scope line, no referral path and no stop rule — while selling one-to-one "mental training" sessions at the end of it.

Iván's call, September 11, 2026: **the guide stops being a mental training guide and becomes a race execution guide.** That is entirely inside coaching scope, needs no external reviewer, and is the more useful document.

*The full merged findings list and the disagreements between the two critiques are not reproduced here — they were working material and the decisions below are what survived.*

## 2. The second finding: `methodology.md` §9 already had the method

The reframe did not invent a document. `methodology.md` §9 already contained a complete race-execution method — the pacing architecture (conservative first half, push from 30k), race-day plan contents, taper psychology, and the four-step debrief protocol — written in Iván's voice, derived from real athletes, and delivered only inside 1:1 coaching. §10 supplied the register for the hardest section: *"No sabemos qué tienes. La decisión la tienes que tomar tú."*

**The mental guide was the detour. This guide is §9 written for athletes.**

## 3. Scope decision — race execution, both halves

Shape: **preparation half + race-day half**, not race day alone. Race-day-only would have dropped the periodised rehearsal calendar, which is the differentiator and the bridge to the coaching sale.

Positioning: the spine is **the decision at kilometre 30**, not pacing advice. Pacing content is free everywhere; the decision architecture for a dead goal is not owned by anyone in this market.

## 4. What was cut, and it does not come back

- **Growth mindset as the load-bearing belief**, and "no es negociable." Macnamara & Burgoyne's meta-analysis: d = 0.05, nonsignificant after publication-bias correction, d = 0.02 in the highest-quality subset.
- **The three pillars** (Confianza / Constancia / Control). This is Sheard's SMTQ, a contested construct presented as what "modern sport psychology" defines. It isn't.
- **CBT and ACT named as therapies**, and the "sistema operativo" framing.
- **The entire "Depresión Post-Carrera" section** — the clinical word in a heading, the invented dopamine/cortisol mechanism, and "abrázala" as the prescription with no duration threshold.
- **The "arrival fallacy" neurochemistry.** A popular-book coinage presented as "neuroquímica básica."
- **The diary's "Resiliencia" and gratitude prompts.** *"¿Qué fue lo más difícil que enfrenté esta semana (en el deporte o en la vida)?"* — the parenthesis is the line between a training log and intake notes.
- **The burnout-prevention claim** and **the body-scan recovery claim** — the latter contradicted by source #63 of the old guide's own bibliography, a Frontiers 2023 null result.
- **Second-person self-talk as a research finding.** The endurance evidence is 16 cyclists over two 10K time trials, ~2.4%, no natural-self-talk control.
- **Everything that made persistence moral:** *instinto asesino*, *micro-abandonos*, the six-week no-missed-session streak, *"un atleta no es débil, está no entrenado."*
- **"Romper la Cinta."** A terminal surge on every rep raises session load beyond prescription and teaches the opposite of §9's own pacing method.
- **The 98-item bibliography** — Reddit threads, Medium posts, app marketing and Headspace copy in one numbered list with real papers, sixteen entries on topics the document never discussed, and no body sentence tied to any source number. Replaced by three sources that are actually cited.
- **The `[TEMPLATE IMPRIMIBLE]` design briefs**, including the "Veredicto: SÍ" editorial notes left in the athlete-facing file. Replaced by four real templates.
- **The "VOS podés" stanza** — violates `brand-guidelines.md` §8 (tuteo, neutral Latin American), settled September 5, 2026.

### What is deliberately NOT added

Both critiques listed body image, weight, disordered eating and exercise dependence as gaps. **Both were wrong about the remedy.** Naming them and routing them is in scope; covering them is not. The guide names them in §Alcance as out of scope and routes them, and adds no content on any of them.

## 5. The reframes — psychology rewritten as coaching

| Old (psychology) | New (coaching) |
|---|---|
| *Reencuadra la ansiedad como emoción* | Start-line activation is a **pacing risk**: it compresses perceived effort, so you will run faster than planned. The fix is a written pace ceiling for km 1–5, not a mantra. §7 |
| CBT *Atrapa, Cuestiona, Cambia* | **Check the story against the data** — the split, the HR, the last gel. §9.2's three questions. This is §10's "data before opinion" applied to the athlete's own head. |
| ACT *Nómbralo para Domarlo* | Cut. What replaced it is naming the **options** (sostener / bajar / parar), not naming the thought. §9.1 |
| *Dominar el dolor* | Hold the prescribed number to the end of the interval. Scope is the rep. §5 |
| Mantras | **Execution cues tied to a named failure** — two, chosen from errors the athlete already has on video. §8 |
| Box breathing as a daily practice | Cut. Warm-up, the existing activation routine and the caffeine protocol handle the flat-athlete case. §7 |
| *Diario de Rendimiento* with life prompts | **The debrief**: body → athlete's version → data → one lesson. §11, copied from §9. |
| Everest / micro-cimas + the streak goal | Block-by-block rehearsal calendar. §5. The streak goal is gone. |
| *Visualización intensiva* | **Physical rehearsal**: the simulation race four weeks out at goal pace, the course study, three readings of the decision playbook. §4 |
| *Desconéctate* (Fase 4) | Kept and expanded — off-season, "la forma es temporal", roles outside sport. §5. The most culturally native move in the old guide. |

## 7. The stop list and the §11 question

`methodology.md` §11 forbids sharing researched medical context — *"not as research, not as caveated context, not at all"* (hardened August 2, 2026). Both critiques recommended printing a medical red-flag list, which collides with that rule as written.

**Decision, September 11, 2026 (Iván): a narrow exception.** A printed stop list is permitted **in published athlete-facing guides only** — never in conversational coaching, never in the AI Coach. The list is sourced to Meijen et al., a sport-psychology expert paper written explicitly for coach delivery, and it prescribes one action: stop and see a doctor.

⚠️ **`methodology.md` §11 needs the dated amendment recording this exception. Not yet written — it is Iván's edit, and the AI Coach system prompt consumes that file.**

The guide's own §9.5 closes with the rule unchanged: *"yo no diagnostico."*

## 8. Sources and the figure that was cut

Three sources, each cited for one specific claim:

1. **Smyth (2021), PLOS ONE** — pacing collapse begins at 29.6 km (men) / 29.3 km (women), sustained 10.7 / 9.6 km; 28% of men, 17% of women; ~2M race records. This is the guide's founding statistic.
2. **Meijen, Brick, McCormick et al.** — the stop-symptom categories, the adaptable goal tiers, and the finding that distraction works at low-to-moderate intensity and fails at high intensity (which is why §8 switches attention by **intensity, not kilometre**). ⚠️ *Citation year/venue unresolved: the Essex manuscript filename says SEPR 2022, retrieved metadata says Frontiers in Psychology 2021. Confirm before the EN/PT versions print a year.*
3. **Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006)** — implementation intentions, 94 tests, d = 0.65. This is what the *si… entonces* playbook is, and why §3.3 insists the trigger be observable rather than felt.

**Cut figure:** an earlier working assumption put the collapse window at "miles 17–22 across 110,000 finishers." No source was found for it. It is not in the guide. The defensible statement is **from ~30 km**, which is what Smyth, Meijen and `methodology.md` §9 independently agree on.

## 9. Register and language

ES is primary and written first. `automation/register-sweep.py` run before shipping. Tuteo, neutral Latin American vocabulary per `brand-guidelines.md` §8. No hype vocabulary, no exclamation marks, no cheerleading — §10's rule, which the old guide broke repeatedly.

EN and PT are written natively from the Spanish, not translated verbatim. Race vocabulary that has to survive the crossing: *el muro / the wall / o muro*, *el corte de tiempo / the cut-off / o tempo limite*, and the framing that **caminar no es fracaso**, which is the sentence the whole §9.4 exists to support.
