# Race Execution Guide — home doc

*Last updated: September 11, 2026*

Home doc for `race-execution-guide/guia-de-ejecucion-{es,en,pt}.md`, the athlete-facing guide to preparing, executing and deciding a race. Owns the scope decision, the cut list, the reframes and the structure contract the generators depend on.

Owns no numbers. Every pacing rule, taper structure and debrief step is a copy of `methodology.md` §9; the pain rules are copies of §7; the scope box is a copy of §14. Move it there first, then here. Owns no fuelling content: `fueling-guide-brief.md` and `methodology.md` §8 own that, and this guide cross-references rather than restating.

## 1. Why this exists

A *Kit de Herramientas Mentales* covered growth mindset, CBT and ACT worksheets, mantras, imagery, box breathing, a body scan, and a section headed "Depresión Post-Carrera". Two independent model reviews, run separately against the same file, both returned unpublishable. The problem was scope, not prose: a performance coach was distributing named psychotherapy protocols and a section about depression under his own name, with no scope line, no referral path and no stop rule, while selling one-to-one mental-training sessions at the end of it.

The nutrition guide had failed the same way a week earlier, but `methodology.md` §8 already carried a fuelling scope line and the guide had breached it. Here there was no mental scope line to breach. That is the harder failure, and it is why §14 was written before the generators ran rather than after.

Iván's call, September 11, 2026: the guide becomes a race execution guide. That is inside coaching scope and needs no external reviewer.

## 2. `methodology.md` §9 already had the method

§9 contains the pacing architecture (conservative first half, push from 30k), the race-day plan contents, taper psychology and the four-step debrief protocol, in Iván's voice, from real athletes, delivered only inside 1:1 coaching. §10 supplies the register for the decision section: *"No sabemos qué tienes. La decisión la tienes que tomar tú."* This guide is §9 written for athletes.

## 3. Shape

Preparation half plus race-day half, not race day alone — race-day-only drops the periodised rehearsal calendar, which is the differentiator and the bridge to the coaching sale. The spine is the decision at kilometre 30, not pacing advice. Pacing content is free everywhere; the decision architecture for a dead goal is not.

## 4. What was cut

- Growth mindset as the load-bearing belief. Macnamara & Burgoyne: d = 0.05, nonsignificant after publication-bias correction.
- The three pillars (Confianza / Constancia / Control) — Sheard's SMTQ, a contested construct presented as settled sport psychology.
- CBT and ACT named as therapies, and the "sistema operativo" framing.
- The "Depresión Post-Carrera" section: the clinical word in a heading, an invented dopamine/cortisol mechanism, and "abrázala" with no duration threshold.
- The arrival-fallacy neurochemistry, a popular-book coinage presented as basic neuroscience.
- The performance diary's "Resiliencia" and gratitude prompts. *"¿Qué fue lo más difícil que enfrenté esta semana (en el deporte o en la vida)?"* — that parenthesis is the line between a training log and intake notes.
- The burnout-prevention claim, and the body-scan recovery claim, the latter contradicted by source #63 of the old guide's own bibliography.
- Second-person self-talk as a research finding. The endurance evidence is 16 cyclists over two 10K time trials.
- Everything that made persistence moral: *instinto asesino*, *micro-abandonos*, the six-week no-missed-session streak, *"un atleta no es débil, está no entrenado"*.
- "Romper la Cinta". A terminal surge on every rep raises session load beyond prescription and teaches the opposite of §9's own pacing method.
- The 98-item bibliography, with Reddit threads and app marketing beside real papers, sixteen entries on topics the document never discussed, and no body sentence tied to any source number. Replaced by three sources that are actually cited.
- The `[TEMPLATE IMPRIMIBLE]` design briefs, including the "Veredicto: SÍ" editorial notes left in the athlete-facing file.

Body image, weight, disordered eating and exercise dependence are named as out of scope in the scope box and routed. Both reviews listed them as gaps; covering them would move further out of scope, not closer in.

## 6. Written from zero

The mid-race decision layer, about 30% of the guide and none of its predecessor: the A/B/C goal ladder with conditions attached and the fourth line for when today is not a race day (§2); the three questions in a fixed order, with the stop list asked first and "no tomes una decisión grande con el estómago vacío" before any decision to quit (§9); dropping one step and recomputing the pace (§9); walking as a pacing tool, with the arithmetic shown so an athlete can redo it at their own pace (§9); the stop list (§9); cut-off arithmetic including the gun-time versus chip-time trap (§9).

## 7. The stop list and §11

`methodology.md` §11 forbids sharing researched medical context, hardened August 2, 2026. Both reviews recommended printing a medical red-flag list, which collides with that rule as written.

Iván's decision, September 11, 2026: a narrow exception. A printed stop list is permitted in published athlete-facing guides only, never conversationally and never in the AI Coach. Sourced to Meijen et al., and it prescribes one action — stop and see a doctor. §11 carries the dated exception and §14 is the home for the whole boundary.

The guide's own §9 does not restate "yo no diagnostico". Rule 2 of the stop list already sends the athlete to a doctor that day; repeating the disclaimer after it reads as protecting the coach rather than the reader.

## 8. Sources

Three, each cited for one claim.

1. Smyth (2021), PLOS ONE — pacing collapse begins at 29.6 km for men and 29.3 km for women, sustained 10.7 and 9.6 km; 28% of men, 17% of women; about 2M race records. The founding statistic.
2. Meijen, Brick, McCormick et al. — the stop-symptom categories, the tiered goals, and the finding that distraction works at low-to-moderate intensity and fails at high, which is why §8 switches attention by intensity rather than by kilometre. Citation year and venue are unresolved: the Essex manuscript filename says SEPR 2022, retrieved metadata says Frontiers in Psychology 2021. The guide cites the URL without a year. Confirm before any surface prints one.
3. Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006) — implementation intentions, 94 tests, d = 0.65. This is what the si/entonces playbook is, and why §3 insists the trigger be observable rather than felt.

An earlier working assumption put the collapse window at "miles 17–22 across 110,000 finishers". No source was found for it and it is not in the guide. The defensible statement is from about 30 km, which Smyth, Meijen and §9 independently agree on.

## 9. The structure contract

The three files are structurally identical by construction, because the parser throws otherwise. Verified September 11, 2026: 1 H1, 20 H2 and 36 H3 in each; 10 tables with row counts `[6,5,7,6,5,5,4,3,10,6]`; 166 paragraphs, 7 quotes, 19 lists.

Shape: H1 title, H2 strapline, `## Alcance`, `## Mensaje del coach`, `## 1.` through `## 16.`, `## Fuentes`. No `# Parte` headings mid-document — the first draft used three and the parser cannot represent them. It was flattened before any builder ran.

`MAGNET_SECTIONS = [2, 9, 14]`: the A/B/C ladder, kilómetro 30, and the si/entonces template. The scope box ships with the magnet uncut, same rule as the fuelling magnet. Headings renumber 1..n in the magnet.

The parser also throws if a second H1 appears, or if section 9 has no warn heading in any language. The stop list is why §11 got its exception; a build that silently lost it would ship a guide telling athletes to push on with no counterweight.

## 10. Register and language

ES is primary and written first; `automation/register-sweep.py` run before shipping. Tuteo and neutral Latin American vocabulary per `brand-guidelines.md` §8. No hype vocabulary and no cheerleading, which is §10's rule and the one the old guide broke most often.

EN and PT are written natively, not translated. Race vocabulary that has to survive: *el muro / the wall / o muro*, *el corte de tiempo / the cut-off / o tempo limite*, and *caminar no es fracaso*, the sentence §9 exists to support.

The scope box says "Soy coach, no psicólogo" rather than "tu coach". Not every reader is a coached athlete — an All-Access subscriber is not — and the possessive claimed a relationship most readers do not have. For the same reason "Lo que yo hago y lo que no" opens with "Con los atletas que entreno": the service is scoped, the boundary is not.
