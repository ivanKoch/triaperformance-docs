Dead bug with band
3 sets · 10 reps · Each side
Band
▾
Lying on back, band above knees, arms pointing to ceiling. Press lower back into floor. Extend opposite arm and leg simultaneously while resisting the band pulling knees together. Slow and controlled — 3 seconds per rep.
Why it matters
The band above knees fixes your foot turn-in pattern (glute med inhibition). If the foot turns in during the rep, stop and reset.

Single leg glute bridge

BW
▾
On back, one foot flat, other leg extended or bent in air. Drive through the planted heel to raise hips. Hold 2 seconds at top — squeeze the glute deliberately, not just the hamstring. Right side gets more volume every session until glute med is reliably firing.
Why it matters
Do this before squats — wakes the right glute so it contributes from rep 1, preventing the hip shift you noticed.

✓
Goblet squat
4 sets · 10–12 reps · 90 sec rest
12 kg
▾
Kettlebell at chest in both hands. Feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out. 3 seconds down, pause 1 second at bottom, drive through heels. The goblet position forces upright torso and deep hip crease — this is what "points to the problem" and makes the glute fire correctly. Go as deep as back allows comfortably.
Priority
The single best exercise for your pattern right now. When 16kg feels easy for 12 reps, move to 21kg.

Bulgarian split squat
3 sets · 10 reps each leg · 2 min rest
12 kg
▾
Back foot elevated on chair or couch, front foot forward. Hold KB goblet style or at sides. Slow descent, feel right glute engaging at bottom. Drive through the front heel to stand. Right leg first every set. These are taxing — full 2 minutes rest between sets is earned.
Why it matters
Primary posterior chain developer. More glute-specific than regular lunges due to the elevated back foot. Right side always first.
Sets (R/L)
✓
Single leg RDL
3 sets · 10 reps each leg · 90 sec rest
16 kg
▾
Standing, KB in opposite hand to standing leg. Slight bend in standing knee. Hinge from hip — back flat, KB travels close to the leg down toward mid-shin. Feel the hamstring load at the bottom. Return by driving through the heel and squeezing the glute at the top. Right side first every set.
Why it matters
Targets the upper hamstring/semimembranosus — the area that fatigues on long bike efforts. Consistent work here directly improves bike endurance.
Caution
Right knee inside-back discomfort has been present. If you feel it during RDL, reduce range of motion slightly rather than stopping entirely — keep the hinge within pain-free range.

21 kg
▾
Stand on a step if available for full range. Hold 21kg KB in same-side hand. Slow lowering (3 seconds down) — the eccentric is where the adaptation happens. Rise on 1 second. The soleus took a hit from FRC hill sessions — consistent calf work prevents recurrence and supports Achilles health through the training block.
Sets (R/L)
✓
Copenhagen plank
3 sets · 25–30 sec each side
BW
▾
Side plank with top leg resting on a chair or bench. Bottom leg hangs free. Hold the position — adductors and glute med working simultaneously. This directly addresses the inner thigh fatigue pattern from long bike efforts.
Caution
Right knee inside-back discomfort — use KNEE as the lever point on the chair, not ankle or foot. This shortens the mechanical arm, reducing adductor load to a manageable level. Progress: knee → mid-shin → ankle as the knee resolves over weeks.

Couch stretch — hip flexors
2 min each side
—
▾
Kneel with shin against wall or couch, other foot forward. Upright torso, squeeze glute of back leg. The psoas attaches directly to your lumbar vertebrae — when this releases after squatting and loading, the lower back decompresses immediately. Non-negotiable post-session.
Why it matters
Most important post-gym stretch for lower back health. The psoas is almost certainly contributing to your remaining back tightness.
✓
Pigeon pose — right side priority
3 min right · 1 min left
—
▾
Right leg forward, shin at angle, left leg extended behind. Fold forward over the front leg. 3 minutes right side — min 1 resisting, min 2 yielding, min 3 real change happening. The piriformis releases under sustained passive load, not under stretching effort. Breathe into it.
Why it matters
Your most important gym-day stretch. The piriformis pattern drives every right-side issue in your training history.
